# Jack Underwood

My name is Jack Underwood, and I’m a senior applied mathemat- ics major at Middlebury College currently completing the Applied Math Modeling senior seminar. My path to mathematics was any- thing but linear (see what I did there?); growing up in Nashville, Tennessee, I was first in love with reading—particularly biographies. One book quickly reshaped my passions, however: Surely You’re Jok- ing, Mr. Feynman, the semi-autobiography of physicist Richard Feyn- man. It began an obsession with problem-solving that has never left. That obsession led me to join a local chess team, teach my- self coding, and even a brief ambition to be a naval engineer—yet mathematics itself never captivated me. I was competent but unin- terested and at times I resented math. That changed in AP Calculus AB, where a dedicated teacher and genuinely compelling problems fi- nally sparked a deeper interest in the subject. I won math awards that year and arrived at Middlebury with a better sense of my inter- ests. 

Still, I wanted to explore academically during the beginning of my college career. As it turns out, that only lasted one semester. My first calculus course was the impetus for my decision to study math; from an exceptional professor, to genuinely interesting problems, and even the camaraderie formed in that class, I knew I wanted to declare as an applied math major. 

Even after this discovery, the journey came with lows. During my sophomore year, I questioned whether I belonged. Coming from an under-resourced school district, advanced mathematics often felt like a discipline built for someone else. But I made a firm decision: as long as math compelled me, I would not quit. I would not let doubt—mine or anyone else’s—override curiosity, and so I persevered. 

As I wrap up my time here at Middlebury, I want to leave a message to my younger self, and to others who are still finding their passion: follow what makes you curious, and build the kind of community where others can do the same. Approaching graduation, I will carry three things mathematics has taught me—rigorous problem-solving, resilience, and the ability to build meaningful relationships—and I am confident they will define whatever comes next.
